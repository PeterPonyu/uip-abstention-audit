## MT29 — Chemistry-stratified value of selective abstention for ML crystal-stability prediction

**Verdict:** incremental (medium confidence)

**Peer papers:**
- Matbench Discovery -- An evaluation framework for machine learning crystal stability prediction (Riebesell et al., 2023, https://hf.co/papers/2308.14920) — defines the exact benchmark (WBM structures, DAF, classification-vs-regression disconnect at the hull decision boundary) that MT29's entire audit is built on top of.
- CHGNet: Pretrained universal neural network potential for charge-informed atomistic modeling (Deng et al., 2023, https://hf.co/papers/2302.14231) — original paper for one of the four frozen MLIPs (CHGNet) whose predictions are audited for chemistry-stratified abstention value.
- MACE: Higher Order Equivariant Message Passing Neural Networks for Fast and Accurate Force Fields (Batatia et al., 2022, https://hf.co/papers/2206.07697) — original architecture paper for MACE, one of the four frozen MLIPs whose hull-margin predictions are stratified by anion chemistry in MT29.
- Orb: A Fast, Scalable Neural Network Potential (Neumann et al., 2024, https://hf.co/papers/2410.22570) — introduces ORB, the fourth frozen MLIP audited in MT29, and reports its Matbench Discovery error reduction that motivates using it as a model under test.
- Evaluating Universal Machine Learning Force Fields Against Experimental Measurements (Mannan et al., 2025, https://hf.co/papers/2508.05762) — independently finds that universal MLIP reliability gaps correlate with chemical-environment/training-representation rather than architecture — the same chemistry-dependent-reliability thesis MT29 tests via abstention.
- Overcoming Common Flaws in the Evaluation of Selective Classification Systems (Traub et al., 2024, https://hf.co/papers/2407.01032) — methodological peer on evaluating confidence-based abstention/selective classification at fixed coverage — same risk-coverage evaluation machinery MT29 applies to the stability decision.
- SelectiveNet: A Deep Neural Network with an Integrated Reject Option (Geifman & El-Yaniv, 2019, https://hf.co/papers/1901.09192) — foundational selective-prediction/abstention (reject-option) paper establishing the risk-coverage trade-off framework MT29 repurposes for hull-margin-based abstention on stability classification.
- MLIP Arena: Advancing Fairness and Transparency in Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials via an Open, Accessible Benchmark Platform (Chiang et al., 2025, https://hf.co/papers/2509.20630) — peer benchmark platform that likewise moves past static DFT-error metrics to surface chemistry- and condition-dependent MLIP failure modes across the same class of foundation potentials (CHGNet/MACE/etc.).

**Industry SOTA:**
- UMA: A Family of Universal Models for Atoms (Meta FAIR, 2025, https://hf.co/papers/2506.23971) — trains a single mixture-of-linear-experts foundation potential on half a billion atomic structures across molecules/materials/catalysts, claiming it matches or beats specialized models with no fine-tuning — the industry benchmark for "one model, all chemistries" that MT29's chemistry-stratified failure result complicates.
- Open Materials 2024 (OMat24) Inorganic Materials Dataset and Models (Meta FAIR, 2024, https://hf.co/papers/2410.12771) — EquiformerV2 models trained on 110M+ DFT calculations claim state-of-the-art Matbench Discovery leaderboard performance (F1 > 0.9, ~20 meV/atom) — the strongest published aggregate discovery-accuracy claim MT29's stratified reliability audit contextualizes.
- MatterSim: A Deep Learning Atomistic Model Across Elements, Temperatures and Pressures (Microsoft Research, 2024, https://hf.co/papers/2405.04967) — claims near-first-principles-accuracy stability and property prediction across the full periodic table and wide T/P range, the closest industry claim to universal (chemistry-agnostic) reliability that MT29's stratified result pushes back against.
- Learning Smooth and Expressive Interatomic Potentials for Physical Property Prediction (eSEN) (Meta FAIR, 2025, https://hf.co/papers/2502.12147) — introduces eSEN, claiming state-of-the-art materials stability prediction by enforcing energy-conservation during MD as a proxy for downstream reliability — a competing industry recipe for "trustworthy" MLIP stability screening.
- Scaling deep learning for materials discovery (GNoME) (Google DeepMind, 2023, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06735-9) — reports 2.2M candidate crystals and 381k newly predicted stable materials from GNN-based screening at massive scale — the flagship industry discovery-acceleration claim whose implicit uniform reliability across chemistries MT29's abstention audit interrogates.

**Gap analysis:** No cited paper combines all three load-bearing elements of MT29: (1) the selective-prediction/reject-option framework (SelectiveNet, Traub et al.'s risk-coverage evaluation critique) applied specifically to (2) the Matbench Discovery hull-stability binary decision using |hull margin| as the confidence signal, measured via DAF/precision-at-coverage, and (3) stratified by anion chemistry with a battery of robustness controls (matched-yield, leave-one-element-out, WBM-round, committee-variance, shuffle-null, BH-FDR/Holm). Matbench Discovery itself defines the benchmark and flags the classification-vs-regression disconnect but performs no abstention analysis. SelectiveNet and the Traub et al. selective-classification paper supply the general reject-option machinery but are domain-agnostic (medical imaging, general ML), not materials-specific. Mannan et al. (2508.05762) and MLIP Arena independently converge on the qualitative claim that MLIP reliability is chemistry/environment-dependent rather than architecture-dependent — this is close in spirit and somewhat weakens novelty of the headline finding — but they establish this via comparison to experimental measurements or benchmark-platform surfacing of failure modes, not via a selective-abstention/DAF-improvement lens with formal multiplicity-corrected statistical testing. MT29's specific contribution — that confidence-based abstention's *value* (not just error magnitude) is chemistry-stratified, with oxides benefiting and halides/intermetallics not, demonstrated across four frozen production MLIPs with an extensive control suite — is a genuine, non-trivial synthesis of existing building blocks rather than a wholly new problem or method. This places it as incremental: a defensible, well-evidenced delta on top of two clearly separable prior threads (the benchmark + the chemistry-dependent-reliability observation) rather than a novel research question or already-published result.
